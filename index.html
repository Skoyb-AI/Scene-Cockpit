💡 What is a scene?

A scene is not just an exchange of dialogue or things happening in a room. A scene is a unit of change. By the end of a scene, a character's situation or state must shift from positive to negative (or vice versa).

  • Goal: Someone wants something specific right now.
  • Conflict: An obstacle (person, environment, dilemma) gets in the way.
  • Shift: The scene ends with a change, new stakes, or an unexpected outcome.

Coaching Tip: How to define scene purpose

Avoid generic ideas like "Show they like each other" or "Explain the backstory." Instead, use the formula: "[Character] wants [X], but faces [Y], causing [State Shift]."

Coaching Tip: Where does this sit in the story arc?

• Hook: Establishes normalcy and grabs interest.
• Inciting Incident: Disrupts the status quo; forces the character to act.
• Rising Action: Obstacles mount; plans fail or become complicated.
• Climax: Highest point of tension where the ultimate decision is made.
• Resolution: The dust settles; reveals the new normal.

Coaching Tip: Choosing your narrative lens

• First Person (I/me): Immediate, intimate, but limited solely to what the narrator observes.
• Third Limited (he/she/they): Tied tightly over the shoulder of one character's senses.
• Third Omniscient: All-knowing narrator who can freely inspect anyone's thoughts.
